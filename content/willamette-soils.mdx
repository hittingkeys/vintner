## Three pits, not three wine styles

Jory, Willakenzie, Laurelwood — those are soil series names, not tasting notes. Each name points at one described hole: what the soil formed from, and how deep that typical pedon goes before it hits a floor. The same three names sit on different landforms because that is where their parent materials are. They are not sprinkled uniformly through the valley, and an AVA name is not a soil map.

Drainage is a different property. Jory and Willakenzie are both well drained, both moderately slow permeability. They are not “fast” versus “slow.” Water-holding and rooting depth are different again; this lesson does not measure those.

Click a colored extent or a type-location pin. The matching pit highlights. Drag the depth line. Each pit keeps its parent material and typical depths together. At this depth: what is here, and has this pit hit a floor?

## Where the names sit

The colored regions are SoilWeb generalized SSURGO series extents (UC Davis), not a soil survey you can site a vineyard from, and not AVA blobs. Pins are NRCS OSD type locations. Jory is described on foothills adjacent to the Willamette and Umpqua Valleys — the Jory layer continues into the Umpqua; pan south. Willakenzie is described on low hills along the valley margins (Spencer Formation along the western margins; Eugene and Fisher Formations along the eastern margins in the southern portion). Laurelwood is described on hills along the northwest margin. The valley floor is a different parent-material story: these three are hillside soils.
